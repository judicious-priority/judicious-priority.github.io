STRATEGIC FUEL ANALYSIS: HELIUM-3 LUNAR TRAJECTORY VS. TRITIUM SAFETY

1. Future Cost Reductions via Lunar Commercial Mining
Terrestrial Helium-3 remains restricted to the nuclear beta-decay of military Tritium reserves. However, its long-term cost curve is projected to decline significantly due to space logistics. The Moon has an estimated 1.1 million metric tons of Helium-3 embedded in surface regolith from solar-wind bombardment. Private operators like Interlune, Black Moon Energy, and ispace are executing prospecting flights. Interlune is preparing its Prospect Moon extraction sensor payload for a 2027/2028 integration window with NASA and Astrolab, targeting commercial return deliveries by 2029. As automated sifting rovers establish supply lines, scaling will drive down prices for aneutronic fusion power plants.

2. Radiological Safety Advantages over Tritium
Opting for a Deuterium-Helium-3 cycle rather than a traditional Deuterium-Tritium matrix provides an inherently superior safety profile:
  Elimination of Neutron Flux: Deuterium-Tritium fusion expels 80 percent of its energy as destructive 14.1 MeV fast neutrons, causing material activation and requiring massive concrete or lead shielding. Deuterium-Helium-3 fusion is primarily aneutronic, yielding energy as non-radioactive charged protons and Helium-4 particles.
  Direct Energy Conversion: Because products are charged ions, they can be magnetically guided directly into solid-state converters to generate electricity with up to 80 percent efficiency, skipping heavy steam turbines.
  Mitigated Risks: Tritium is radioactive with a 12.3 year half-life, creating biological hazards and weapons-proliferation tracking overhead. Helium-3 is completely stable, non-radioactive, produces zero long-lived nuclear waste, and reduces stray secondary neutron emissions down to roughly 1 percent.
